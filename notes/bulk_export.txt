Digital Signal Processing for Acoustic Monitoring

=== SLIDE 2 ===
Why DSP matters for acoustic sensing
- Microphone arrays deliver continuous waveforms, not numbers
- Most faults show up as changes in the spectrum, not the waveform
- A small set of estimators covers most monitoring needs

=== SLIDE 3 ===
Sampling and aliasing
- Sample rate must exceed twice the highest observed frequency
- Aliased energy is indistinguishable from genuine low-frequency energy
- Anti-alias filtering is cheaper than fixing a bad recording

=== SLIDE 4 ===
Windows and leakage
- A finite record is always a windowed observation
- Rectangular windows smear tonal peaks across bins
- Hann and Blackman windows trade resolution for sidelobe suppression

=== SLIDE 5 ===
Power spectral density
- PSD distributes signal power over frequency
- Welch averaging reduces variance at the cost of resolution
- Use consistent units ? V/Hz is the usual choice for microphones

=== SLIDE 6 ===
Filters in one slide
- IIR filters are cheap but can ring and drift
- FIR filters are linear-phase and unconditionally stable
- Choose the shortest filter that still meets the rejection spec

=== SLIDE 7 ===

=== SLIDE 12 ===
Spectral centroid
- A one-number summary of where the spectrum sits
- Bright broadband noise has a high centroid
- Weight each bin by its power

=== SLIDE 13 ===
Computing the spectral centroid
- Normalise by the total bin power
- Guard against an all-zero spectrum
- Values are monotone in the brightness of the signal

# spectral centroid: ?_k f_kP_k / Σ_k P_k
weighted = sum(f[k] * P[k] for k in bins)
total    = sum(P[k] for k in bins)        # ?_k P_k  0
centroid = weighted / total              # bins ? Hz

=== SLIDE 14 ===
Time-difference of arrival
- Two microphones observe the same event at different times
- Cross-correlation peaks at the inter-channel lag
- Lag converts to metres once the speed of sound is known

=== SLIDE 15 ===
Estimating the inter-channel delay
- Search lags symmetrically around zero
- The peak index is the delay in samples
- The angular error is bounded by one half sample

# cross-correlation lag: peak of r_xy(?)  delay
lags = range(-max_lag, max_lag + 1)
peak = argmax([r_xy(tau) for tau in lags])
delay_s = peak * 1000 / fs               # ? error  0.5 sample

Tracking a slowly varying delay

=== SLIDE 18 ===
Bearing estimation

=== SLIDE 19 ===
Reporting levels in decibels
- Decibels are ratios, so always state the reference
- Magnitude spectra use the factor twenty
- A tiny epsilon keeps the logarithm finite
